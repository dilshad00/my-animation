Imagine making your computer faster… with nothing but a pencil.

Sounds ridiculous, right?

But back in the early 2000s, this was a real trick used by PC enthusiasts.

Some AMD Athlon and Duron processors had tiny electrical connections on their surface called L one bridges.

AMD cut these connections to lock the CPU's clock multiplier.

But here's where things get crazy.

Graphite inside an ordinary pencil can conduct electricity.

Enthusiasts discovered they could use it to reconnect those tiny circuits, unlocking the processor's multiplier.

That meant they could push the CPU beyond its original clock speed.

One famous example? A cheap AMD Duron six hundred could potentially reach speeds around one gigahertz.

That's a massive jump for a budget processor.

But it wasn't guaranteed, and stability and cooling still mattered.

And no, this won't work on your modern Ryzen or Intel CPU.

Today, processors are far more sophisticated.

But imagine explaining to someone that a pencil once helped overclock a computer.

Would you believe it?
